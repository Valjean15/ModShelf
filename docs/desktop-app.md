# The desktop app

Install ModShelf from the [latest release](https://github.com/Valjean15/ModShelf/releases/latest) and start it from the
Start menu, the Applications folder or the AppImage. From source, run `dotnet run --project src/ModShelf.Desktop`.

Every setting named here in `code`, such as `updates.interval`, is described in [settings.md](settings.md).

## The main window

The main window has a sidebar of the screens, Library, Updates, Browse and Downloads, with **Settings** at its bottom, and the one selected. The button at the top of the sidebar hides the screens' names. **Search** under it, or Ctrl+K (⌘K on macOS) on any screen, opens quick search: with nothing typed, your last 10 searches that opened something and the screens; typing finds games, the one with most mods in the library first (opening in the Library when the library has its mods, in Browse otherwise), the library's mods (opening their page), the screens, and the settings that mention it (opening their category, or a game's own settings), names matched as the game picker matches them. ↑ and ↓ move, Enter opens, Escape closes; **Clear** beside "Recent searches" forgets them. The window comes back as it was left: its size and place, maximized or not, the sidebar open or collapsed, and the screen last open; a window last on a screen that's gone, or larger than its screen now, comes back whole on a screen that's there. **Settings**' badge counts what to look at there: a newer ModShelf to install, and the accounts that need attention, as when a site refused the account or downloads of a site failed for want of one; its tooltip says how many. While the app runs, queued downloads go on, finished ones go into the library, and the watched mods are checked for updates every `updates.interval`.

## Closing the window, the tray and the logs

The first time the window closes, the app asks whether to keep running in the tray or quit, and remembers the answer (`window.closeToTray`, also on the Settings screen). In the tray, its icon opens the window, and its menu has **Open ModShelf**, **Check for updates now**, which says what it found in a notice, and **Quit ModShelf**; its tooltip counts the updates found. On Linux, the tray needs a tray host, which KDE and most desktops run and GNOME has with the AppIndicator extension; without one, closing the window quits. Starting the app again while it runs brings its window back instead of opening a second one; each library folder gets one app. It uses the same library database, settings, accounts and environment variables as the command line, so a library made with one shows in the other. One of them at a time runs the downloads: while a `download` command runs them, the Downloads screen says so, and the app starts its queue once the command has finished. When the library database can't be opened, the app says which file and why in a window, and closes. It logs to a file a day in a `logs` folder beside the database, such as `desktop-20261029.log`, keeping a week of them. Each run's logs start with a line saying which ModShelf it is, its process ID and the process that started it, and a library database command that takes a second or more, such as one waiting for another process's lock, is logged as a warning, with what it was but never its values.

## First-run setup

A new library, with no mods and no account linked, starts with a short setup over the main window: where the library goes, linking an account, and whether ModShelf opens Nexus Mods' `nxm://` links. Each step is the same as on the Settings or Accounts screen, where it can be changed later. **Finish** or **Skip setup** ends it for good; closing it otherwise shows it again at the next start.

## Finding games and mods by name

Wherever you type to find a game or a mod by its name (the Library's filters, the game picker, Settings search, `mod games` and `mod_games`), each word you type must start a word of the name, in any order. Case, accents, apostrophes, quotes and other punctuation don't count, so a word may run on across the name's words: "stalker 2" finds S.T.A.L.K.E.R. 2: Heart of Chornobyl, "dragons dogma" Dragon's Dogma 2, "pokemon" Pokémon and "spiderman" Spider-Man. A Roman numeral from II to XXX is its number too: "final fantasy 12" finds Final Fantasy XII.

## Using more than one site

Once two sites are in use, those the library has mods of and those with an account linked, each game, mod, update and download from a mod site shows its site's name beside it, and the Library has a **Site** box beside the games' order, which shows one site's games and counts them alone; it starts on **All sites**. Direct links aren't a site. With Nexus Mods alone, nothing shows.

## Library

The Library screen shows the games of the mods you downloaded or watch, each with a cover, how many mods it has, the updates found for them, the room their files take on disk, archived versions included, and when they were last checked; a cover shows the game's banner from its site, saved like mods' pictures, and the game's initial on a colour of its own until the site answers, or when it gives none. A box filters the games by name, and they're ordered by most mods, the default, by name, by most room on disk, or with updates first. Games hidden in their settings are left out, with **Show hidden games** to show them too. 

Clicking a game shows its mods in a table: each mod's name and author, your version, what to know of it (that it's gone from its site, an update, files missing from disk, or that it's watched only or not watched), its files, the room they take, and when its site last updated it. A box filters them by name or author; toggles show only the mods with an update, the watched ones, those with archived versions or those with files missing, and, while a mod is gone from its site, those gone from their sites, each counting its mods, and with several on, the mods they all say; they're ordered by name, the default, with updates first, by when their site last updated them, by size, or by when they came into the library. Each mod has a box to select it, and **Select all shown** selects them all; **Update selected** downloads the selected mods' updates, and a mod a filter hides is no longer selected. **Watch**, **Unwatch** and **Remove**, beside it, each counting the selected mods it would change, watch them, stop watching them (a mod without files then leaves the library), or take them out of the library, their files staying on disk; none asks their site. **Endorse**, for a game of a site where you can endorse, such as Nexus Mods with an account linked, counts the selected mods you haven't endorsed, asks first, naming them, and endorses them on the site one at a time; it has no **Undo**, since abstaining is a mod page's button. Which mods you endorsed is read from the site once a run, when one of its games opens; then, while a mod of the game isn't endorsed, the toggles also offer **Not endorsed**. The bar at the bottom offers **Undo** for unwatching and removing, all the mods at once, and what couldn't be done to a mod, such as one whose download isn't finished, is said once, the others done and unselected. **Library** at the top goes back to the games, and the gear beside a game's name opens its own settings. 

Clicking a mod's name opens its page in place of the list, as Browse shows it, with what the library has of it: its files, where they are, whether any is missing from disk, its archived versions when asked, each SHA-256 to copy, and in each file's **…** menu, **Show contents…**, for a `.zip`, `.7z` or `.rar` on disk, and **Delete…**, which deletes it from disk after asking; which of the site's files are updates for yours; and when it came into the library and was last checked. When the site can't be reached, the page shows the mod's saved details, says why, and offers **Try again**. 

Each mod's **…** menu also opens its page there or on its site, or its folder, watches or unwatches it, **Rescan** looks for its files placed by hand, and **Remove from library** takes the mod out of the library, leaving its files on disk. Each mod's **…** menu also has **Pin at this version**, which keeps its updates from being offered, counted or downloaded by **Update all**, its status saying "Pinned at 2.7" until **Unpin**; **Add a note…**, a note of several lines its page shows; and, once updates are skipped on the Updates screen, **Offer skipped updates again**. 

After **Remove from library**, **Unwatch**, **Pin at this version**, **Unpin**, a note emptied, or **Skip** on the Updates screen, a bar at the bottom of the screen says what happened, with **Undo**, for ten seconds, one change at a time; undoing puts the mod back as it was, with its updates, pin, skipped updates and note, and its files as the library has them now. Deleting a file isn't undone, and still asks first. 

**Rescan** at the top of the screen rescans every mod, and says what it found. **Recent activity** beside it shows what happened to the library's files over the last year, by day, the newest first: each file downloaded, with the version it replaced, each old version archived or deleted, each file a rescan found, each mod removed, and each mod that left its site or came back, with its game; **Show** narrows it to one kind, and a mod's name opens its page, whose way back returns to the activity. It shows the newest 1,000. 

When a check finds a watched mod gone from its site, hidden or removed there, it isn't counted as failed: the mod says "Gone from *site*" first among what to know of it, its tooltip since when, its page says your copies of its files may be the only ones left and that its details are the last ones seen, and a notice says which mods left their sites, for the games that show notices. Its site is asked about it again once a week, and once its site lists its files again the mark goes. On a mod's page, a file of yours that its site no longer offers, or lists only as archived, is tagged **Only copy**; and keeping the latest old versions moves such a file into `_archive` rather than deleting it. 

**Export list…** at the top of the screen writes the library as a list of its mods and their files to a file you choose, by the sites' IDs, never by download links, which expire: each mod's site, game, ID, name, page, watching, pin and note, and its files but the archived versions. On a game's page it writes that game's mods, or only the selected ones. **Import list…** brings a list into the library, here or on another computer: **From a list file…**, or **From a collection link…**, which asks for the link of a collection's page on its site, such as a Nexus Mods collection's, and reads it at the revision the link names, or the latest. Its dialog first says what importing would do, from the library alone: the mods new to the library and those in it already, those of sites ModShelf can't reach here, and the files it would download; for a collection, also the files its site no longer offers, and what it needs from elsewhere, such as a script extender, each with **Open page**, since ModShelf never downloads those. **Watch the mods and download the listed files**, the default, can add the files marked optional, and download a mod's newest main file in place of one its site no longer offers; **Only watch the mods** downloads nothing. **Import** asks first when a site's requests look too few, as **Update all** does, then imports a mod at a time, with **Stop**, and says what it did and what it couldn't, each in a sentence. A mod the library has keeps its own watching, pin and note. Nothing is installed, and a collection's install steps aren't run: its files go into the library, as downloads from it do. 

**Import archives…**, beside it and on a game's page, brings another mod manager's downloads of a game into the library, such as Mod Organizer 2's or Vortex's: it asks which game, unless a game's page is open, as **Add a game…** in Settings does, then the folder. Its dialog says how many archives (`.7z`, `.zip` or `.rar`, not in subfolders) are there, and about how many requests identifying them takes; **Identify** asks first when that looks like more than the site has left, as **Update all** does, then reads each archive once and says what each is: a file of one of the game's mods, by Mod Organizer 2's note beside it, its name, or its content, which Nexus Mods looks up by MD5; in the library already; a copy of another there; or unknown, and why. **Copy into the library** copies the identified archives where a download of each would go, watching the mods new to the library, and says what it did, and what it couldn't copy, such as for want of room. **Stop** ends either step; importing the folder again goes on where it was. The archives are only read: nothing in their folder changes, and nothing is installed. 

**Free up space…**, beside it and on a game's page, lists what could go from the library's folders, every game's or the one open, asking each mod's site at most once which files it still offers. Its dialog shows each group with its total and a box that ticks all its files that can go: **Archived old versions**; **Duplicates**, files the library has twice, each saying which copy stays; **Files of mods not watched**; and **Files that match nothing**, files in the mods' folders that the library doesn't have and that match none of the mods' files on their sites. Each file shows its name, its mod and folder, and its size. A file that may be the only copy left is kept, its box unticked and greyed, saying why: its mod left its site, or the site no longer offers it or couldn't be asked; so is a file that turns out to be one of the mod's files on its site, which **Rescan** adds. Archived old versions and duplicates start ticked. **Delete selected (size)…** asks first, then deletes the files one at a time, as deleting a file from its mod's page does, and says what it did, and what it couldn't. Deleted files go for good, not to the trash, and **Recent activity** lists each. **Stop** ends listing or deleting.

**Show contents…** opens a dialog of what the archive holds, read where it is, extracting nothing: its folders and files as a tree, each with its size once unpacked, a folder's being its files' together, under a line counting them. The top folder is open when it's the only thing at the top, and each folder's arrow opens and closes it; **Open or close every folder** does what it says. The archive's readme is chosen and shown beside the tree as it opens; choosing any other text file, such as an `.ini` or a `.txt`, shows it there, up to its first 256 KB, saying when that's not all of it. An archive, or a file in it, that needs a password says so, as does a damaged one; ModShelf never asks for passwords. Only the rows of open folders are made, so an archive of thousands of files stays quick, and the window can be made larger.

The games and mods show as Settings › General › **Previews** says: as rows with thumbnails, the default, as cards with large pictures, or as rows without them; in a narrow window, a table puts its numbers under the names. Rows and cards are built as they scroll into view, so a game of hundreds of mods opens at once. A game with previews of its own, from its settings, shows them on its page. 

## Updates

The Updates screen lists the updates found for the watched mods by game, each heading with the game's icon from its site, but a pinned mod's and the ones skipped, a mod's new optional files among them, under a line that counts them and says when the mods were checked and when the next check is, with **Check now**. Each update says its change of version, such as "1.3 → 1.4", and shows its version's entry in the mod's changelog, read the first time you open the screen, a few mods at a time, and kept while the app runs. **Download** puts an update into the library beside the file it replaces, and **Skip** leaves it out until a newer one comes; **Update all**, at the top or on a game's heading, downloads them all, or the game's, one after the other, and a game's mods in the Library have it on their heading too. With a free Nexus Mods account, each file then waits for you on the Downloads screen. Both screens follow the library as it changes, and the badge on Updates counts the updates; a hidden game's are left out of both.

## Pictures and mod details

Mods' pictures come from their sites, and their details, such as which picture is theirs, are saved in the library database, so the screens show them at once and offline; saved details are read from the site again once they're a day old (`details.maxAge`). The pictures are saved in a `pictures` folder beside the database, up to 512 MB (`pictures.maxSize`); the ones shown least recently go first. An adult mod's picture is blurred, with the site's blurred thumbnail when it has one, unless `pictures.adult` says `hidden` or `shown`. Nexus Mods gives its mods' thumbnails and blurred thumbnails through its GraphQL API, which ModShelf asks first for a mod's details; a mod it doesn't give is read from the older API, without thumbnails, so its thumbnail is its full picture, made small.

## Browse

The Browse screen searches a game's mods on Nexus Mods, mod.io and CurseForge, which need a linked account, and on GameBanana, DEG Mods and Thunderstore, which need none. Nothing is listed until you pick a game in the game picker, which leaves out hidden games: Browse opens on the game you picked last, in this run or the one before (kept in `ui-state.json`), as soon as its site lists it, and the × on its chip takes it away, back to nothing listed, until you pick another. With two sites or more, the box to the picker's left narrows it to one site's games, **All sites** by default, each then without its site's name, and only that site is asked for games by name. Choosing a site while another site's game is chosen chooses its game the library has the most mods of, or else opens the picker to choose one; a game of another site that quick search opens moves the box to that site. The box's choice is kept in `ui-state.json` between runs. Under its field, **On this PC (Steam)** lists first the games Steam installed on this computer, as their games on the sites: the one of the same name, or the one chosen for it in Settings › Games, each once, and only the chosen site's; a name two games of a site share lists both, each with its key, and choosing one keeps it for that installed game. Typing finds them by their names or the installed games'. Then **Your games** are the library's, but those, the one with the most mods first, each saying how many; typing finds the sites' other games too, those whose names start with it first, 50 at most, each site's as it answers: Nexus Mods', mod.io's, DEG Mods', Thunderstore's and CurseForge's from their lists of games, and GameBanana's, too many to list, by GameBanana's own search, once a word typed has 3 letters; with nothing typed, it offers Browse's last 10 searches, which search again in the chosen game, and are kept in `ui-state.json` between runs, until **Clear** beside them forgets them. ↑ and ↓ move from game to game, Enter chooses, and Escape closes the list; the game chosen shows as a chip in the field, its site beside it when the games come from two sites, and its × takes it away. 

Choosing a game starts the order, the time and the page size as the game's own settings say, when it has some: until you search, the game's page shows three rows of mods, **Trending** (from the site's own list), **Recently added** and **Recently updated**, each with **Show all** for every mod in that order but Trending; clearing the words searched for brings the rows back. A row the site couldn't list, such as before its account was linked, is asked for again when Browse is shown again. 

Search by words; **Filters** opens a card over the results with the order, one of fourteen (by relevance, endorsements, downloads, unique downloads, last update, publishing, size or name), the mods updated in the last 24 hours, week or month, one of the game's categories, an author, adult content, and 20, 50 or 100 a page. What's changed there searches once you **Apply**; **Close** or Escape leaves what was applied, and **Reset** puts the card back to how the game starts. Each filter applied but the order and page size shows as a chip under the search, such as "Category: Armour", whose × removes it at once, with **Clear all**; the button counts them ("Filters · 2"), and the results say their order. **Load more** lists the next page. The card offers only what the game's site can search by, in its own words, such as Nexus Mods' endorsements; a game's saved order or page size its site lacks starts on the site's own. Each game keeps its filters while the app runs: choosing it again brings them back. 

GameBanana and mod.io mark their mods with adult content but can't leave them out, so ModShelf does (DEG Mods' and Thunderstore's are left out as it searches), unless **Include adult content** is ticked in **Filters**; a page without them lists fewer, and **Load more** goes on after them. Sorted by size, each mod says its size; by newest, when it was added; by last update, when. A link to a mod in the search box opens that mod. 

Each mod has a box to select it, and **Select all shown** selects every mod listed; **Download selected** puts the newest main file of each into the library, and says how many it queued, which were in the library already, and what couldn't be queued. A new search starts with none selected. The view switch beside **Filters** shows the results as cards with each mod's picture, the default, built as they scroll into view, as rows with thumbnails, or as rows without pictures; a chosen game with previews of its own shows those, and the switch changes the game's. 

Clicking a mod opens its page: its picture, as the previews say, its details and category, its files by section, with **Download** into the library and **Save to a folder…** for each, its changelog's latest entries, newest first, with the rest when asked, and what it needs, where the site says (a mod it needs opens on its page in ModShelf, anything else in the browser). When it needs other mods, each file's **Download** has **Download with requirements…** beside it, and what it needs has **Download what's missing…**: a dialog lists each requirement, ticked to download its newest main file when the library lacks it, or marked as in the library, already downloading, off the site (with **Open page**, since ModShelf never downloads those) or why it can't be downloaded; **Include their requirements too** adds what those mods need, down to three levels, each mod once. **Download** asks first when a site's requests look too few, then queues the file and the ticked mods into the library, and says what it queued. The site's list can be wrong or out of date, and nothing is installed. It can be watched in the library, and tracked and endorsed on Nexus Mods: **Endorse** says whether you endorsed it already, as "Endorsed", and clicked again abstains, as the site's own button does; Nexus Mods refuses a mod you haven't downloaded, and the page says so in its words. One in the library can be rescanned or removed there. 

**Tracked mods** lists the mods of the game you track there, and **Subscribed mods** those you subscribe to on GameBanana, with your profile linked, or on mod.io, with an access token linked, which ModShelf can only read: change them on the site's website. Beside either list, **Watch all (5)** watches in the library each mod listed that it doesn't watch yet, one after the other, then says how many it watched and why any couldn't be.

## Downloads

The Downloads screen takes a link to a mod or a file: **Download** puts it into the library, and **Save to a folder…** into a folder you pick. A line under the heading says what's under way, how fast, and what waits for you. Once a speed limit is set, a second line says it, "Limited to 2.00 MB/s" or "Not limited (2.00 MB/s when on)", with a switch that turns it on and off, keeping the speed; a running download takes the change within seconds. It lists every download, newest first, with its progress, speed and time left, and the buttons to pause, resume, retry or cancel it; the tabs above the list show all of them, or those under way, waiting for you, failed or finished, each with its count. Cancelling a download with bytes saved asks first. **Clear finished** removes the done and cancelled downloads from the list; their files stay. 

A download Nexus Mods wants started on its website shows **Open on Nexus Mods** and **Paste link…**: start it there with the download for mod managers, copy the `nxm://` link its button holds, and paste it; or, once ModShelf opens `nxm://` links (Settings), the download goes on by itself. 

A download another site sends to a page, such as MediaFire's, or a file only DEG Mods' own server has, shows **Open the page**: download the file there, and ModShelf takes it from your browser's downloads folder once the browser finishes it, the file with the download's name (in any case, or with the number a browser adds, such as " (1)") or with the SHA-256 the site gives, and moves it into the library; the download completes as if ModShelf had downloaded it. Only files saved while the download waits count, and ModShelf watches the folder only then. **Choose the file…**, on any download waiting for you, Nexus Mods' too, hands it a file you pick. A file is checked against the site's hash when it gives one: one that doesn't match is left where it was, and the download keeps waiting, saying so. 

While files wait for you, a strip above the list counts them, and **Open the next page** opens the page of the one that has waited longest, one page a click. The badge on Downloads counts the downloads waiting for you, to be started or because they failed. Updates and Downloads show their mods' thumbnails when Settings › General › **Previews** says **Thumbnails**; by default, they show rows without them.

## Accounts

Accounts are Settings' first category, where Settings opens: a row for each site, under "Needs an account" (Nexus Mods, mod.io, CurseForge, whose own API key each user makes on the CurseForge for Studios console), "Optional" (GameBanana) and "No account needed" (DEG Mods, Thunderstore and direct links, each saying what ModShelf does there). A site's row shows the account's picture, or an empty ring with none linked, its level in a tag, its name and what's left of the requests that run out soonest, such as "Kestrel · 498 of 500 requests left this hour", or "Not linked"; and **Link…** or **Check**. **Details** opens it for the rest: the day it was linked, the site's API requests left, as it last said, the ways to link an account, and **Unlink**. A row says when its account needs attention, which Settings' badge counts. Everything that pointed to the Accounts screen, such as Browse's **Go to Accounts**, quick search and `modshelf://accounts`, opens Settings › Accounts. 

While fewer than 10% of a site's requests for the hour or the day are left (`quota.warnBelow`, in Settings › Updates), a calm strip at the top of the window says how many and when they renew, warm once they're used up; its × hides it until they renew. Before **Check now**, **Update all**, **Update selected** or **Download selected**, when the work would likely take more requests than the site has left, the app says so and asks whether to go on: a check takes one request for each game and one for each mod never checked or due a full check, and a download two. 

A button for each way the site offers links an account: an API key pasted into a box that hides it, with a button that opens the site's API keys page (on mod.io, your API key or a personal access token, both from its API Access page); a code the site emails; or approving ModShelf in the browser, with **Open page again** and **Cancel** while it waits; or, for GameBanana, **Link your profile**, by your member name or your profile's link, read only. **Check** asks the site who the account is now, which also brings the API requests left up to date; ModShelf asks the site nothing there otherwise. **Unlink** removes the account and its stored credentials, after asking. Each time the accounts are shown, they're listed again, such as one linked from the command line meanwhile.

## Updating ModShelf itself

The installed app updates itself from ModShelf's GitHub releases, leaving out pre-releases; the 0.9 betas took them too. As it starts, it looks for a newer release, and when there is one, the Settings screen's badge and its "About ModShelf" card say so, with what's new. **Download** downloads it, and **Restart to update** quits and starts the new release in its place; nothing changes until then, however often ModShelf starts. **Check for updates** looks again, and "Look for a newer ModShelf when it starts" (`app.checkForUpdates`) turns the look at start off. A build from source doesn't update itself. To try updates against releases of your own, set `ModShelf__UpdateFeed` to a folder `build/pack.ps1` packed into. On Windows, a click on one of ModShelf's notices opens the Updates screen, and uninstalling gives the `nxm://` links back to the app that had them. On Linux and macOS, give them back on the Settings screen before deleting the app.

## Settings

The Settings screen changes what `settings set` does, in categories after **Accounts** (above): **General** (the theme, closing the window, previews, pictures and mod details), **Library** (its folder, your browser's downloads folder, the folder pattern, old versions, the download speed and **Rescan the library**), **Updates**, **Links**, **Agents** (AI agents and the MCP server), **Games** (each game's own settings) and **About**. **Search settings** shows the settings that say every word typed, from every category, and **Games** with the games whose names match; choosing a category, or Escape, ends the search. 

**Choose another folder…** moves the library, with its files after asking, and **Use the default folder** moves it back; games with folders of their own stay where they are. **Your browser's downloads** is the folder a download waiting on a site's page takes the browser's file from: **Choose another folder…** picks another, and **Use the system's Downloads folder** puts the default back. The folder pattern shows where a sample file would go as you type, and is saved with **Save**. **Download speed** limits ModShelf's downloads together: **Limit downloads to** turns it on, with a speed in KB/s or MB/s, from 16 KB/s to 10,000 MB/s, saved as you change it; changing the unit keeps the speed. Unchecked, the speed is kept for the next time. Files your browser downloads aren't limited. **Previews** says how each screen shows its mods, the Library, Browse, Updates and Downloads: as **Rows**, **Thumbnails** or, in the Library and Browse, **Cards**; Browse's view switch changes Browse's too. 

**Games** lists the games' own settings, then, at the bottom, **Games on this PC**: the games Steam installed on this computer, each with its games on the sites, by the same name or as you chose ("Nexus Mods: Skyrim Special Edition, chosen"), and where they were found and how many have a site's game ("Steam: 42 games installed, 30 with a site's game."). ModShelf reads Steam's own files for them: its library folders and each game's manifest, on Windows where the registry says Steam is, on macOS in `~/Library/Application Support/Steam`, and on Linux in Flatpak's folder, `~/.local/share/Steam`, `~/.steam` or Snap's folder; it writes nothing there, and asks no account or key. A game still downloading, and Steam's own tools such as Proton, are left out. Each game's **Choose…** chooses its game on a site, from every site's games, keeps that a site has none (**None here**), or lets its name decide again (**Match by name**); the choices are kept in `settings.json`'s `installedGames`, by the game's store and ID, such as `steam/489830`, then each site's game key, or `null` for none. They're read again each time Settings › Games and Browse show, every site at once, a site that can't list its games said. 

Each installed game is a compact row: its name, a pill for each site with its game (the site's name alone when the game has the installed game's name, "*Site*: none" for a site chosen to have none, and a warm "*Site*: *N* games" when several share its name), and **Choose…**. A box finds an installed game by its name or its games' names, and **Without a site's game** shows only those no site has a game for yet; the list scrolls on its own, building only the rows shown, so hundreds of games stay quick. Above them, the games' own settings list the library's games and those with settings of their own, with a box that finds one by its name and how many there are ("3 of 124 games"), in a list that scrolls on its own and reads a game's cover once its row shows, and **Add a game…** finds another by its name among the sites' games not listed yet, or adds one by the key its site names it by, such as `stardewvalley`, when the sites can't list theirs. A game's page says where its files are, how many and how much room they take: in the library's folder, or a folder of their own. **Choose…** picks another, then says what would move, from where to where, the room there, the files there already and any missing from disk, and **Move** moves them, with how far it is; **Stop** puts back every file moved. A drive without room enough, or a folder the files can't go to, such as one inside another game's, is refused, with **Choose another folder…**, and a move that stopped says why, with **Try again**. **Move back to the library** moves them back, and a game without files takes the folder at once. The page shows each of its other settings using every game's, which it says, until **Override** gives the game its own, which **Use default** gives back: its folder pattern, old versions, update checks (whether its mods are checked with the other games, and how often), notices, previews (how its mods show on its Library page and in Browse), and how Browse starts for it; and **Hide** leaves it out of the Library, Updates and Browse, its mods still checked. 

What happens to old versions, how often to check for updates, whether to show a notice, and whether the app is light or dark are saved as you choose them; the theme changes at once. "Pictures and mod details" shows how much room the saved pictures take, with **Clear**, and sets the most they may take, how long mod details are kept, and whether adult mods' pictures are hidden, blurred or shown. 

**Links from websites** says which app opens Nexus Mods' `nxm://` links, the ones its **Mod manager download** buttons hand over, and Thunderstore's `ror2mm://` links, from **Install with Mod Manager**, each on its own: **Open them with ModShelf** makes it ModShelf, remembering the app that had them, such as Vortex or Mod Organizer 2, or r2modman, and **Give them back** puts that app back. While ModShelf opens them, a click on the website brings the app forward on the Downloads screen: a download waiting for that file goes on, and otherwise the file goes into the library, whether the app was running or not. On macOS, only the installed app can open them. The screen also says where the settings are kept, and warns when no keyring protects the credentials. Each time it's shown, it shows the settings as stored, such as previews changed in Browse or a change from the command line.
